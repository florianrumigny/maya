# Inspiration research

Use for the guided branch routed by [discovery.md](discovery.md). Produce an evidence-backed territory map before direction cards or complete visual proposals.

## Collect references

1. Start from the stable product frame and identify tensions worth exploring: speed versus contemplation, precision versus warmth, or density versus openness, for example.
2. Build a search plan across relevant families: graphic movements, typography, editorial design, objects, materials, fabrication processes, architecture, cultural references, and adjacent interfaces. Explore at least four families, including two outside software; note why omitted families would add little to this brief.
3. When browsing, image search, or connected collections are available, retrieve and inspect real references. Prefer attributable artifacts from designers, foundries, publishers, museums, architects, manufacturers, or the products themselves. Follow promising results beyond the first search cluster and examine the actual work and its context.
4. Keep at least eight useful references across the chosen families, from multiple creators or sources. Broaden the search if these mostly repeat one aesthetic. Counts establish coverage; they do not substitute for distinct principles.

### Optional web source: Inspo MCP

When available, use [Inspo MCP](https://inspomcp.dev/mcp) after the product frame is stable for web-oriented retrieval and inspection:

- use `recommend`, `search_screens`, or `find_examples_for_macrostructure` to locate real screens and structural examples;
- use `get_screen`, `get_design_system`, `compare`, or `get_site_pages` to inspect candidates, measured visual observations, comparisons, or site flows.

Record the canonical source URL, site or screen slug, credit, and relevant tool evidence in the existing source ledger. Treat captures, extracted `DESIGN.md` content, and reference JSX as research evidence or throwaway exploration material; they do not become MAYA or project rules, copy targets, or production code. Verify high-salience observations against the capture or source. Inspo can cover web and software references, but it does not replace the requirement to explore two outside-software families. If it is unavailable, follow the existing browsing, image, or local-material path.

For each retained reference, record:

```md
Reference ID:
Artifact, creator, and context:
URL or local asset path:
Evidence: [visually inspected | text-only | user-provided | unverified recollection]
Family:
Observed principle:
Product transfer: [what this could change in hierarchy, rhythm, interaction, or expression]
Limits: [what relies on the original medium or context]
```

Separate observations from proposed transfers. For example, borrow a publication's layered reading order to organize dense information; its cover layout is not an interface specification. Preserve cultural context and distinguish a reusable organizing principle from an identity-specific symbol.

If retrieval is unavailable, inspect supplied or local material. If that cannot meet the coverage above, present a provisional map with coverage gaps and unverified leads clearly labeled. Keep recalled or generated examples separate from researched sources and carry these evidence limits into the guided cards.

## Group territories

Cluster references by transferable organizing logic, rather than shared colors or style labels. Build 3-5 distinct territories, each supported by at least two references and a product-relevant thesis. References may support more than one territory only when the borrowed principle differs.

Present each territory with:

- an ID and a name expressing its organizing idea;
- its reference IDs with links or inspected excerpts;
- the principles to transfer and their likely product application;
- the meaningful distinction from neighboring territories;
- the main tension or risk to test.

Keep the source ledger alongside the territory map in exploration notes so guided cards can point back to it. A style label such as “Swiss” or “brutalist” remains a search coordinate; the territory states what its evidence could make this product do or communicate.

## Research gate

Continue to discovery's direction cards only when:

- the family and source coverage above is met, or retrieval is unavailable and the available material has been inspected with coverage gaps explicitly documented;
- every territory traces to its references and names a transferable principle;
- territories differ in organizing logic, not just surface styling;
- the territory map has been presented to the user before complete visual proposals.

If the map collapses into one aesthetic, search the missing families before proceeding. Shortlisting the resulting direction cards belongs to discovery.
